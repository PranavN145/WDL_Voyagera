Must Experience

Before You Leave India

Three experiences that every traveler carries home — permanently.

01

Ganga Aarti, Varanasi

Thousands of lamps, chanting priests, and the oldest living city on earth — at 5 AM, the Ganges becomes something beyond words.

02

Overnight Train Journey

A sleeper bunk, chai vendors at every stop, strangers who feed you and tell you their life story. India unfolds best at 80 km/h.

03

Kerala Backwaters

A houseboat drifting through emerald canals, toddy coconuts, and silence so complete you hear your own breathing. The south hits differently.

Backpacker Guide

Real Talk. No Fluff.

Everything you actually need to know before landing in India.

Daily Budget
₹800 – 2,000
Hostel bed + street meals + local transport. ≈ $10–25/day
🛡️
Safety
Stay Aware
Generally safe. Watch your bag in crowds. Trust your gut. Locals are your best security.
🌤️
Best Season
Oct – March
Cool, dry, and festival-rich. Avoid May–June: extreme heat, especially in the north.
🚂
Transport
Train First
Indian Railways connects everything. Book sleeper class. Auto-rickshaws for cities. Buses for mountains.
🎒
Backpacker Vibe
Overwhelmingly Addictive
Loud, colorful, spiritual, exhausting, and unlike anywhere else. Most travelers stay twice as long as planned.
📶
Connectivity
Excellent 4G
Jio or Airtel SIM at the airport. ₹300/month for unlimited data. Coverage everywhere except deep Himalayas.
Local Moments

Skip the Guidebook

The real India is in the gaps between tourist attractions.

Chai with Strangers

Every roadside dhaba is a living room you were never invited to but always welcome in. Order chai. Stay for two hours.

Morning at the Sabzi Mandi

At 6 AM the vegetable market is a shouting, color-soaked chaos. No tourists. Pure India.

Auto-Rickshaw at Midnight

Wind in your face, city lights blurring past, a driver blasting Bollywood at full volume. This is how cities are felt, not seen.

Neighborhood Cricket

A taped-tennis ball, a gully between buildings, eleven strangers who will adopt you into their team on the spot.

Platform Life

India's railway platforms are civilizations of their own. Vendors, families, sadhus, students — everyone passes through these few square metres.

Unlimited Thali Lunch

₹80. Unlimited refills. Dal, sabji, rice, roti, pickle, papad. The family-run dhabas never disappoint; the five-star restaurants always do.

Food Culture

Eat Like You Mean It

Indian food isn't a cuisine. It's 28 different cuisines fighting to be your favourite.

Pani Puri

Six hollow shells of pure tamarind-spiced water. One explosion per bite. The only queue worth standing in.

Street Food ₹20–40

Masala Kullad Chai

Cardamom, ginger, milk, sugar, and a country's entire social life poured into a small clay cup. Drink six a day. Minimum.

Essential Ritual ₹10–20

Dum Biryani

Slow-cooked over two hours, layered with spices that have no translation. The Hyderabadi vs Kolkata argument has no winner.

Regional Icon ₹150–350

South Indian Thali

A banana leaf, seven dishes, unlimited rice, coconut chutney, and sambar so good you'll forget every restaurant you've ever been to.

Full Meal ₹80–200
Off the Map

Places the Guidebooks Miss

The India that most tourists never reach. The India that backpackers never stop talking about.

Hidden Gem

Hampi, Karnataka

Ancient Vijayanagara Empire ruins scattered between surreal granite boulders. Rent a bicycle. Get lost for three days. Guest houses cost less than a pizza.

RuinsBoulders₹400/night
Hidden Gem

Spiti Valley, Himachal

4,000 metres above sea level. Buddhist monasteries older than the concept of tourism. Zero mobile signal. Silence so complete it becomes a sound.

High AltitudeBuddhistOff-Grid
Hidden Gem

Gokarna, Karnataka

Goa's quieter, more spiritual sibling. Cliffside temples, hammock shacks, a beach you share with maybe twelve people. The way coastal India used to be.
